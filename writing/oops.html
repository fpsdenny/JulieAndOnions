Oops

first published 2022 · moved here

Writing Essays Poems

On mistakes, accidents, mishaps and fuckups.

You are working as a programmer, going through your usual day managing the servers you are responsible for in the building. Troubleshooting code, and trying to get ahead of problems. You are making a correction in a critical file, and upload it before realising there is a small syntax error in what you've written. You work at NASDAQ, and in an instant a vast swathe of the stock market ceases to process orders. Oops. What do you do?

This example was drawn from a book by Michael Lewis, "Flash Boys". It is a true story, and a great book - for lack of memory I have probably fudged the details - nevertheless the programmer in question immediately acted and fixed the problem and the markets were back up in less than a minute. The book goes on to say, unsurprisingly, the higher-ups were rather unimpressed and wanted to see heads roll for such a devastating error. But the supervisor, made another suggestion to them. Do you not want to employ the programmer who would immediately act to resolve the issue? In such an intense position maintaining servers which are live 24/7 handling important financial data, there are few men who could keep their cool knowing millions of dollars in missed transactions are racking up every single second, while they sit and read JavaScript in a dimly lit server room that most of the traders involved don't even know exists. The programmer, a nerd for complex systems, goes on to beg the question of if it could be considered a mistake at all. He elaborates that in the maintenance of complex systems, one knows that things are bound to break at some point. So many moving parts will invariably produce an error at some point in time, so those well versed in this subject know well that the only way to get ahead of this, is to break it yourself and fix it.

Trying to explain this to the bosses at NASDAQ is another can of worms, but most of us will never have to contend with that peculiar problem. However ALL of us will make mistakes. Many, many, many mistakes... Which will incur consequences of all shapes and sizes throughout out lifetimes. When you think about the context of your own life as a complex system, the statement that it will eventually "break" seems very befitting for most of us. We consider ourselves unlucky to be on the receiving end of seemingly all the universe's problems, totally indifferent to the blistering pace of our lives as they pile up like mad before us. What is a human to do?

Now to the suggestion that you should deliberately go create problems at your life, most would roll their eyes and close this blog. They will scoff as though I am fool, and go on to live out their disastrous lives as all of us do, but with an attitude that will leave them poorer in spirit for every day they get out of bed. To the brave few who read on, perhaps even dip their toes in the waters I speak of, let me ask you a question. If we look at the history of our species, where can we find the men who go deliberately to create problems in their lives? I would suggest, that they can be found from cover to cover in our history books. Men and women who were looked at with disbelief, shock and dismay as they shared their ideas with the rest of the world. That is, right up until they succeeded, and made history in the process. People still laugh at Kanye West while he architects a commercial empire out of an absolutely historic career as a musician, and they will probably only cease to find it funny when he buys their suburb and puts his initials on their house. How many of the people who scoff at Trump have had their name in giant gold letters on a multi-billion dollar piece of real estate? Consider that maybe there's a reason for that.

There is only one constant we will encounter in our lives, which is change. Change can be difficult, as we are made uncomfortable and forced to leave behind the beliefs, feelings, maybe even places and people we hold close to our hearts. There is however, one very positive thing which will always, without fail, come about from change. I would argue the single most positive thing that exists in the realm of existence, and it is absolutely everywhere. That thing is LEARNING.

Experiences are valuable for one thing, and it is the knowledge and wisdom we can gather from them. We might think of certain moments in time very dearly for the way they made us feel, the memory they contain. Those moments are a precious part of the human experience, but from the perspective of every individual, right up to society as a whole, that is not the reason they are valuable. How many stupid things as a kid did you not have to do, because your parents, or their parents, did it and dealt with the consequences? Most children fail to heed such warnings, but in the more dire instances good cynical parents know how to tell a story that will keep hands away from hot stove tops for a whole lifetime. Because every single human being on this planet has made innumerable mistakes, and whether they know it or not, are much better for it. What separates the wheat from the chaff in adulthood, is who has learned how to learn and who has not. Because these integral happenings are unavoidable, but drawing lessons from them and moving on with a greater deal of understanding is a choice that each of us must make. A choice that must be made at every second of every day, as life throws the kitchen sink at our poor unarmed conscience.

Nobody likes making mistakes. Fucking up fucking sucks and we all know it. Getting chewed out by your boss, doing an oil change twice, or taking a bite of a salty cookie are not by any means our most fond memories. But they are the things that make us better employees, better mechanics and better cooks. If you are going to judge someone for a mistake, you had better do so knowing that the clock is likely already ticking quickly towards the moment where you are in a position they might do the same to you. None of us can escape life, so you may as well embrace it. That means embracing change, embracing mistakes and embracing learning. These are the only things that matter in our short lives, as it is not WHAT WE DO in our lives that will change the world, but WHAT WE TEACH OUR CHILDREN.

So be decent, and understand the consequences of your actions, but know that you are born on this Earth with an irrevocable license to fuck around. This essay has been deliberately published as a first draft because it is the message that is important and nothing else. If I made a mistake, not only can I learn from it but I can open the WordPress text editor and correct it. I won't though because that would defeat the purpose of this very meta concluding paragraph. I believe this alone is the beauty of life, and why forgiveness is the only virtue which matters. It is right to be in pursuit of perfection, but only if you understand you will never reach it. Go take a tumble, or two, or three. Maybe a whole lifetime's worth. I will meet you at the bottom of the hill and we will share in the wealth of knowledge that comes from it.

Tomorrow is the most important thing in life. Comes into us at midnight very clean. It's perfect when it arrives and it puts itself in our hands. It hopes we've learned something from yesterday.

John Wayne
