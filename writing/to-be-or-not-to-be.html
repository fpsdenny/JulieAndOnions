To Be, or Not To Be

first published 2023 · moved here

Essays Poems

There is a very old story, that goes something like this; monkeys once upon a time swung from trees to collect bananas, when one day they came down to Terra Firma for reasons unknown, perhaps encountering a somewhat familiar fungus snack along the way. In the blink of an eye, they find themselves steering bison-sized metal contraptions to massive warehouses, where fresh bananas are placed on display every day by monkeys with uniforms and name tags. Somewhere along the line, nature started a new game. A game with the same rules and the same field, yet somehow things turned out very... different. A great English poet, left a mark on his species for hundreds of years by composing some of the finest works we know of on man's oldest folly. While a child learns quickly from touching a hot stove, man will choose to place himself in the fire as many times as he can endure, before his short life ends. Somewhere in the missing pages of human history books, everything important and relatable happened. It is not just in the acts of Shakespeare where love and war are woven so tightly together, they appear as one. The game that makes our world so damn crazy, started long before man put pen to paper, and will perhaps endure after he has gone. Many a time in a cave, a locked chamber and under a desk in the oval office, history was made.

Other beings of the planet Earth appear to be stuck in some kind of cycle, competing endlessly against one another for territory and mates. Survival is the best possible outcome, a transitory state, as the floor of a kitchen can be clean. Scrubbed daily or not decay may never cease, but interesting happens in nature when a question is posed. The observation of clean can be split in two by asking; 'Tile, or Hardwood?' The future becomes dependent upon a decision, and the variables corresponding to whatever activity will take place on the floor in question. Undeterred by logic, activities placing great importance on such decisions all over Earth on a daily basis. Human DNA mutates at the same rate as the rest of nature, successful just as rarely, but somewhere along the way a paradigm shift took place. A new, more efficient process. Using nothing but our thoughts, higher orders of complexity slowly began to be applied to our environment. Humans found a new way to evolve, a new way to change. A seed planted, which would slowly grow from stone axes and cave paintings, into wooden shelters, dyed paint on canvas and high velocity ballistics. It has been said that once upon a time fire was stolen from the sky, defying the gods to keep his fellow man warm. There must come a time where we look back on this exchange and ask ourselves, who stole what?

Something which have never been observed anywhere in the universe, exerts a large force on the relationship dynamics of one species. Elsewhere in nature, partners are chosen on a seemingly less... esoteric basis. Planets and other astrological bodies rotate around one another for very logical reasons, and the same could be said of lion mating selection. There is not one, but multiple dimensions to human mate selection each operating across a unique spectrum to each individual. These are the imaginary dynamics of personality, who we are and what we see in others. Forming some abstract field, influenced by the past but changing constantly with time as learning is gathered through experience. These variables become real when emotions are presented, chaotic and illogical, but not without reason. The path which any given relationship will take from the moment of two individuals meeting is non-linear, dynamic, and extremely complex for this reason. Coupled with a sort of chemistry that is just as unpredictable and explosive as we imagine, this is the game in question. Taking place somewhere unknown in time and space, a bond has the power to move our lives as the moon does the tides. No less real than the feeling of being pulled to the Earth, from the moment a spark is lit in the place we call our heart, time is changed. Far from the first author I am to writing about love, rather I would like to call to the attention of the reader the share of literature in all forms, on this subject. There is no doubt in my mind, aliens upon observing human behaviour would make no mistake this was the basis for our whole existence.

Many a time there has been a man who lived for love, and was willing to die for it. One of the oldest stories we know of, told of a man who was charged a crime of hatred, crowned in thorns and executed for it. As far as we know he never wrote anything, but his story was made apart of history because of the way he talked to, and treated others while he walked the Earth. There are many titles, and names that could be attributed to this character, though we may never truly know who he was how many lives he has lived. We will call him Jesus. Any number of disputations could be made about the authenticity, and importance of the scriptures in question, alas does there remain any physical evidence of love? Alexander the Great? The Flood? Time slowly turns history into legend, eroding knowledge and wisdom in its wake. Whether or not there was a real Romeo, or a real Juliet, the story has endured for the truth enclosed in its acts, the words and movements and the emotions we associate with them. Not the truth to be proved but to be felt, because this is the sort of truth that changes our shape and becomes apart of us. The reader is encouraged to consider what one piece of media they might choose to take in the event of a crisis. With every answer, the apparent likelihood of our far-away descendants having a functional understanding of organic chemistry diminishes. The story of Jesus Christ has resonated with so many, and been carried through many a shifting tide, because he spoke the truth. Not by instruction, or by prescribing a way of thinking, but by telling stories. To any willing to listen, he was willing to share his wisdom, allegedly from God, yet shockingly human in its presentation. Often overlooked, it should be recognised most of Jesus' friends (not disciples) were disabled or sick, social outcasts and tax collectors. In his day, scorned by the priests, the wealthy and powerful. Yet he persisted, ever careful in his words and movements. The 'Sermon on the Mount', at once casting the seeds of prophecy and watering the garden which they were to grow in. Call it love, call it war, call it politics, the Son of God did what he said he would. Truth, is dynamic and changing in our lives. At the same moment, someone can be telling the truth, and not telling the truth. Honesty has less to do with lying than it does with knowing.

No matter how much knowledge of the past we ascertain, it will never be known how much has yet been lost. Questions are destined to forever stand taller than answers, and certainty does little but cast a shadow on the patterns to be observed. There is almost so little we know about the past attachment to any part of it bears its own burden, knowledge is power and with power comes responsibility. It is not technology or understanding of the universe that will give man peace, to be free of the mistakes of the past requires understanding of oneself. As a ball of snow grows larger as it rolls, by the fire of love and the coldness of war with each passing generation we gather and become apart of the burdens of the past. Old enemies and friends alike, weigh heavy in our hearts as we pass from moment to moment. When time hands down a decision to us, we are too quick shoot from the hip, along the same firing lines that have developed deep grooves over centuries of use. Somewhere in the universe, maybe even our own bodies and minds, thoughts take place. Invisible, and weightless, with the power to move mountains, to make or break a whole species at once. We, the poor fools tossed deep into human history stumble around planet Earth with no sight or understanding of the wolves circling ever closer. Save for the crumbs left by one man, possessing a wealth beyond imagination. A wealth so grand the devil himself was helpless to seek it. History has placed him on a pedestal not for what he owned, but for what he gave away. A price was paid in spirit, to turn and face the glaring eyes and snarling teeth. In turn, came a prize. The key to the future, a curse wrapped in gold ribbon. Is it better to have loved and lost? Or never to have loved at all?
